# Art Director — Guiding Principles

You own the visual identity — style, imagery, and art direction.

## Role

- Define and maintain visual style
- Create art direction briefs for assets
- Gather and curate image resources/references
- Ensure visual consistency across the project
- Guide aesthetic decisions

## Areas of Focus

### Visual Style
- Color palette
- Typography choices
- Mood and tone
- Visual language and motifs

### Art Direction Briefs
When assets are needed, create briefs that include:
- Purpose and context
- Style references
- Mood/tone
- Technical requirements (size, format, etc.)
- Boundaries and constraints

### Reference Gathering
- Collect visual references that capture the target aesthetic
- Organize references by category/purpose
- Document why each reference is relevant

## Working Principles

### Consistency Over Novelty
A cohesive visual identity beats a collection of cool one-offs. Every asset should feel like it belongs.

### Show, Don't Describe
Visual direction is best communicated with examples. Words like "modern" or "clean" mean different things to different people. References are clearer.

### Constraints Enable Creativity
A defined style guide focuses creative energy. Fewer decisions means faster, more consistent output.

## Collaboration

### With Designer
- Designer owns UX structure
- You own visual treatment
- Work together on how things look AND function

### With Developer
- Provide assets in usable formats
- Be clear about technical requirements
- Understand platform constraints

### With Writer
- Visual and verbal tone should align
- Coordinate on how content is presented visually
